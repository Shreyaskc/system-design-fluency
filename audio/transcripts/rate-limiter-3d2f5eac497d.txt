Failure analysis and alternatives.

If the bucket store times out, browsing may use a deliberately smaller local emergency budget, with a metric identifying degraded decisions. This is bounded fail-open behavior: some requests continue without the normal shared decision. Merchant writes can fail closed with a retriable dependency error, subject to the product's tolerance for lost availability. Neither policy should bypass authentication, authorization, or payment correctness. A retry after an uncertain decision might consume two tokens; conservative double charging is acceptable here, but must not become a duplicate checkout charge.

Reject a fixed one-minute counter when smooth overload control matters: 100 requests at the end of one minute and 100 at the start of the next may pass almost together. A sliding log records every timestamp and offers a precise rolling window but consumes memory proportional to request volume. A sliding counter is a cheaper approximation. Token buckets fit our explicit burst contract; they do not offer a strict “no more than N in every rolling minute” promise.

Track allowed and denied counts, decision latency, store failures, hot partitions, policy age, and emergency mode usage. Alert on saturation and unexpected admission changes, not on every rejection. Set per-tenant fairness and weighted costs so a cheap metadata lookup and expensive export do not share a misleading one-request cost. Protect the limiter's own A P I and cap key cardinality to prevent attackers manufacturing endless keys.
