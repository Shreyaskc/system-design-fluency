Invariant.

Precise definition: A predicate over system state that is true initially and preserved by every permitted state transition.

Plain English: A rule the system must never break, including during competing requests and recovery.

Analogy and its limit: A shop cannot hand out more numbered collection tickets than it has allocated goods; unlike a physical counter, software copies can disagree.

Concrete example: If stock begins at five, accepting two reservations of three violates the nonnegative-stock invariant. Protect the decrement where authoritative state changes.
