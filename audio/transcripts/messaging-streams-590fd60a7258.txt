8. Make asynchronous work replayable and bounded . Backpressure and poison messages.

Backpressure and poison messages.

Backpressure slows or rejects producers when consumers cannot keep up. At 250 arrivals per second and 150 completions per second, backlog grows by 100 per second. A large queue postpones overload; it does not create capacity. Bound queue size, prioritize critical work, limit worker concurrency, and protect downstream services. Track oldest-message age alongside queue depth because users experience delay, not abstract message counts.

A poison message repeatedly fails due to data or code that retries cannot fix. Retry transient failures with a bounded schedule, then isolate the item in a dead-letter queue with reason and safe inspection tools. Assign an owner and replay procedure. Replaying an entire backlog after a bug fix must preserve deduplication and downstream rate limits. Otherwise recovery becomes a second incident.

Practice: a consumer crashes after updating its projection but before acknowledging. What must happen on replay?.

The projection update and processed-event record commit together. On replay the unique event identifier reveals prior completion, so the consumer acknowledges without repeating the update. If those writes are separate, a crash between them can still double-count or skip work.

Exit artifact: draw the crash points before and after each commit, publish, external call, and acknowledgment. For every point, state whether replay loses, duplicates, or safely repeats the intended effect.
