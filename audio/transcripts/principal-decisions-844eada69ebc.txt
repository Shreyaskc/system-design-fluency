Assess evidence, not page completion.

Score each attempt from zero to three on requirement clarity, quantitative reasoning, data correctness, failure analysis, tradeoff depth, and communication. Zero means absent; one means named without mechanism; two means coherent under stated assumptions; three means defended under a changed assumption with a recovery or migration plan. Keep the original attempt so improvement is observable. Reading completion and quiz scores are useful study signals, but neither certifies mastery or guarantees an interview outcome.

Final oral drill: your preferred database is unavailable for six hours. What do you do?.

First clarify whether this is a new-design constraint or an incident. During an incident, preserve invariants, stop unsafe writes, expose pending outcomes, use tested recovery, and communicate measured impact. For a new design, revisit requirements and compare alternatives by transactions, query patterns, recovery, team skill, and migration. Naming a different product without reconstructing its guarantees is not a complete answer.

Exit artifact: maintain three independently attempted cases, one implemented concurrency exercise, one failure rehearsal, and one written decision record with a rejected alternative. Ask a peer to challenge assumptions and record the feedback. Treat remaining uncertainties as a practice backlog with concrete experiments, not as an excuse to add unmotivated components.
