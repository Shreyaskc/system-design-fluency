Scale and nonfunctional goals.

Assume 100,000 daily shoppers and 0.2 checkout attempts per shopper per day: 20,000 attempts per day, or 0.2315 per second average. A 100-fold promotion peak gives 23.15 attempts per second overall. This modest aggregate does not remove contention: 2,000 simultaneous requests may target the same final 100 boxes. Model hot-item contention separately from average throughput.

Assume five minutes of active reservations at 23.15 per second during a sustained peak: approximately 6,945 active reservations. At 1 kilobytes per reservation, raw active records are roughly 6.95 megabytes. Twenty thousand orders per day at 2 kilobytes each produce 40 megabytes per day before indexes, events, and replicas. Transaction contention and correctness dominate this exercise more than storage volume.

Target reservation responses below 300 milliseconds at the 99th percentile in-region, excluding the external payment interaction. Set a proposed 99.9 percent monthly reservation availability target, but never silently oversell to meet it. During an inventory partition, decline or defer affected reservations. A charge can remain pending when the outcome is unknown; showing “failed” prematurely encourages harmful retries. Maintain an auditable state history and keep sensitive payment data with the provider.
