LESSON 13 / 27 · ORIGINAL TEACHING EXAMPLES

12. Practice principal-level decisions and demonstrate mastery

Listen to this chapter →

Move from a plausible diagram to an accountable choice

A principal engineer explains why a design fits a business, how teams will change it safely, and which evidence would overturn the choice. Corner Market has grown from a city pilot into several independently operated markets. A recommendation to split the application must name the bottleneck: conflicting ownership, release coupling, scaling pressure, regulatory boundaries, or operational blast radius. “Microservices scale” is not a decision argument. A modular application can preserve clear ownership until separate deployment has a concrete benefit.

An architecture decision record captures context, options, choice, consequences, and revisit triggers. Keep the consequence specific: one store owns inventory writes, so distant shoppers pay an extra network trip; in return the last-item invariant is easier to protect. A useful revisit trigger is measured checkout latency exceeding the agreed objective for a sustained interval, rather than a vague intention to “scale later.” Include migration cost, staffing, and recovery practice alongside infrastructure capability.

Practice case: search is late, checkout is correct

Prompt: catalog updates appear in search several minutes late, and product leadership asks for instant search and instant checkout everywhere. Clarify the observed lag distribution, which fields need freshness, geographic scope, and whether “instant” means a specific percentile. Assume product descriptions may lag sixty seconds, deletion from public search must propagate within that bound, and price is revalidated at checkout. The first task is to locate delay: outbox age, broker lag, indexing throughput, refresh timing, or client cache.

Do not replace the search engine before measuring the pipeline. If indexing workers are saturated, scale them within storage capacity and apply backpressure; if invalidations are missing, fix event reliability. Serve authoritative details when a result opens and handle deleted items gracefully. For a hard removal promise, a query-time suppression list or authoritative permission check may be required while indexes catch up. That adds a dependency and must itself meet availability and latency requirements.

Reveal a strong answer and a weak answer

A strong answer separates search discovery freshness from checkout correctness, proposes measurements at each boundary, and identifies the smallest compatible fix. It states what users see during lag and how removal requests are verified. A weak answer adds a cache to an already stale path, or promises globally instant indexing without a latency budget and failure policy.

Practice case: shared platform versus team autonomy

Prompt: three teams each built a job scheduler; leadership proposes a single company-wide scheduler. Clarify task volume, deadlines, execution duration, isolation needs, retry semantics, and incident ownership. Shared infrastructure can remove duplicate maintenance but concentrates failure and couples migrations. Separate control-plane functions, such as schedule storage and dispatch policy, from execution environments where tenant workloads run. A control plane manages configuration and coordination; a data plane performs the actual user work.

Propose an adoption path: standardize task identifiers and result states first, onboard a low-risk workload, verify fairness and recovery, then migrate critical tasks with dual observation. Keep tenant quotas and resource isolation so one customer's backlog does not starve another. Define who owns a misbehaving task, who operates the scheduler, and who can pause a tenant. Reject a forced simultaneous rewrite because it creates a large irreversible coordination event without proving the platform's fitness.

Reveal the important tradeoff

A shared scheduler is valuable when it offers a better reliability contract and lower total operating effort than the existing systems. It is costly when teams need incompatible execution guarantees or no team can operate it. A principal answer proposes evidence, staged adoption, and clear ownership rather than equating standardization with universal centralization.

Choose a learning path

A beginner should complete foundations one through five, implement the low-level exercises, and solve URL shortening before adding replication and partitioning. A backend practitioner should begin with the estimates and transaction exercises, then attempt correctness-heavy reservations, payments, and scheduling. A data or AI architect should still complete request-path and transactional reasoning, then emphasize streams, analytics, feature freshness, and model-serving failure modes. A principal interview path adds migration, multi-region ownership, cost, organizational constraints, and adversarial follow-ups to every worked case.

Use deliberate practice rather than reading every sample first. Attempt a prompt for forty-five minutes, record assumptions and unresolved questions, then open the concealed answer. Change one condition: tenfold traffic, an unavailable region, a malicious tenant, or a stricter invariant. Explain which part of your design changes and which remains justified. Repeating the same diagram from memory demonstrates recall; adapting it with explicit consequences demonstrates transferable understanding.

Assess evidence, not page completion

Score each attempt from zero to three on requirement clarity, quantitative reasoning, data correctness, failure analysis, tradeoff depth, and communication. Zero means absent; one means named without mechanism; two means coherent under stated assumptions; three means defended under a changed assumption with a recovery or migration plan. Keep the original attempt so improvement is observable. Reading completion and quiz scores are useful study signals, but neither certifies mastery or guarantees an interview outcome.

Final oral drill: your preferred database is unavailable for six hours. What do you do?

First clarify whether this is a new-design constraint or an incident. During an incident, preserve invariants, stop unsafe writes, expose pending outcomes, use tested recovery, and communicate measured impact. For a new design, revisit requirements and compare alternatives by transactions, query patterns, recovery, team skill, and migration. Naming a different product without reconstructing its guarantees is not a complete answer.

Exit artifact: maintain three independently attempted cases, one implemented concurrency exercise, one failure rehearsal, and one written decision record with a rejected alternative. Ask a peer to challenge assumptions and record the feedback. Treat remaining uncertainties as a practice backlog with concrete experiments, not as an excuse to add unmotivated components.
