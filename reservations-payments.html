LESSON 21 / 27 · ORIGINAL TEACHING EXAMPLES

The last basket: inventory, reservations, and payments

Listen to this chapter →

Worked interview • business invariants • 50 minutes

Exact prompt and business contract

Exact prompt: “Corner Market sells limited bakery pickup boxes. Design reservation and checkout so two shoppers cannot buy the last box, retries do not duplicate a charge, and payment failures do not strand inventory forever.” The central invariant, a rule that must always hold, is that confirmed plus active reserved units never exceed sellable inventory for a stock item. Payment correctness is a second invariant: one order must not accumulate multiple unintended successful charges.

  • Assume each checkout contains stock from one merchant; cross-merchant carts are out of scope for the first version.
  • Assume a reservation lasts five minutes, but payment attempts started before expiry need explicit reconciliation before stock is released.
  • Assume the payment provider supports idempotency keys and status lookup. Its precise key retention and webhook behavior must be checked before implementation.
  • Assume authorization holds funds and capture requests settlement; availability and timing depend on the chosen provider and payment method. We do not build a card network or store card credentials.

Scale and nonfunctional goals

Assume 100,000 daily shoppers and 0.2 checkout attempts per shopper/day: 20,000 attempts/day, or 0.2315/second average. A 100-fold promotion peak gives 23.15 attempts/second overall. This modest aggregate does not remove contention: 2,000 simultaneous requests may target the same final 100 boxes. Model hot-item contention separately from average throughput.

Assume five minutes of active reservations at 23.15/second during a sustained peak: approximately 6,945 active reservations. At 1 KB per reservation, raw active records are roughly 6.95 MB. Twenty thousand orders/day at 2 KB each produce 40 MB/day before indexes, events, and replicas. Transaction contention and correctness dominate this exercise more than storage volume.

Target reservation responses below 300 milliseconds at the 99th percentile in-region, excluding the external payment interaction. Set a proposed 99.9% monthly reservation availability target, but never silently oversell to meet it. During an inventory partition, decline or defer affected reservations. A charge can remain pending when the outcome is unknown; showing “failed” prematurely encourages harmful retries. Maintain an auditable state history and keep sensitive payment data with the provider.

Use integer minor currency units and a currency code; do not calculate money with binary floating point. Obtain prices from trusted server state and store the quoted order amount. Define rounding and tax rules separately. Create or lock the scoped request record in the same transaction as the reservation/order effect. A concurrent identical key waits for the durable result or receives an explicit in-progress response. Lock the order before creating a payment attempt, and enforce at most one live attempt per order (for example a partial unique index over order_id for pending/unknown attempts). A different client key must not bypass that order invariant. Reconcile an unknown attempt before permitting another charge attempt. A request hash prevents one idempotency key being reused with different items. A uniqueness constraint is enforced in storage, not by an earlier “does this exist?” query that races.

Progressive design: keep stock atomic

Begin with one transactional database for a merchant's inventory and orders. A transaction updates stock only if sellable - reserved - sold >= requested_quantity, then inserts the reservation and order. Check affected row count. For several stock items, lock rows in a deterministic SKU order, verify all quantities, and commit all or none. Consistent locking order reduces deadlocks; the application must still retry a deadlock-aborted transaction safely. Do not read availability, release the connection, and later subtract: both buyers could read one remaining unit.

For 100 boxes, reserving 3 sets reserved to 3 and leaves 97 available. Confirmation moves 3 from reserved to sold in one transaction; availability stays 97. Expiry moves 3 out of reserved and leaves sold unchanged. Every transition checks the current reservation state. Expiring twice cannot release 6 units. A cache may show approximate availability for browsing, but the transactional conditional update decides whether checkout succeeds.

A durable state machine makes permitted transitions explicit: HELD → PAYMENT_PENDING → CONFIRMED, or HELD → EXPIRED; PAYMENT_PENDING may become CANCELLED only after the payment outcome is resolved. Persist an attempt before calling the provider. Use its stable key for retries of that same attempt. If the response is lost, query or reconcile the original attempt instead of creating a new key and another possible charge.

Where a transaction stops

The inventory database and external provider do not share our local transaction. Use a saga, a sequence of durable steps with compensating actions when later steps cannot complete. Authorize funds, atomically confirm stock and order if the reservation is still eligible, then capture according to the business contract. If stock confirmation cannot complete after authorization, void the authorization when supported. If capture happens but finalization fails, reconcile and either complete the order or refund. Refund is a new business action that may take time and fail; it does not erase the original charge.

An authorization-first workflow needs a policy for capture failure after confirmation: retain the order in a recoverable pending state, retry within a bounded window, then cancel and release or return stock according to merchant policy. Reserving inventory during payment uncertainty reduces immediate availability but avoids selling the same box to someone else. Describe this tradeoff to the interviewer; there is no magic ordering that eliminates all external uncertainty.

Insert order events into an outbox in the same transaction as the business transition. A publisher sends unsent events and may send twice after a crash; consumers deduplicate using event ID. Publishing directly before commit creates phantom events, while publishing only afterward without durable intent can lose events. Webhooks can also be duplicated or arrive out of order. Authenticate provider signatures, deduplicate provider event IDs, and reconcile against permitted state transitions and authoritative provider status rather than trusting arrival order.

Failures, alternatives, and operating model

An expiry worker conditionally expires only eligible held reservations. It cannot blindly release PAYMENT_PENDING stock at five minutes: a charge might have succeeded during a timeout. Track an uncertainty deadline, look up provider status, and escalate stuck cases with operational visibility. Use the database's time for expiry comparisons. Test the exact boundary where expiry and confirmation race, plus crash-after-provider-success and duplicate-webhook sequences.

Reject a cache lock as the sole overselling defense: lease expiry can let another process enter while the first still runs. Reject separate inventory writes followed by order writes without a transaction or documented compensation. Reject a blanket claim of “exactly-once payments”; the relevant guarantee is stable request identity plus provider deduplication, reconciliation, and constrained state transitions.

Track reserved-age distribution, payment-pending age, stock invariant violations, authorization-to-capture lag, reconciliation backlog, and duplicate-key conflicts. Log correlation IDs and provider references without storing secrets. Restrict refund and manual-adjustment privileges; audit operator actions. Reconcile totals against provider settlement records on a scheduled basis because callbacks alone are not a financial ledger.

Scale by merchant when merchants own disjoint stock. A single hot SKU remains serialized at its correctness boundary; shard counters only with an explicit stock-allocation scheme whose allocations sum to the actual stock. Multi-region writes should route a stock owner to one authoritative region or use an appropriate strongly coordinated store. Cross-region independent decrements cannot safely enforce one remaining unit during a partition. Evolve cross-merchant carts into per-merchant reservations with explicit partial-failure handling, rather than pretending the original single-database transaction extends globally.

Practice: the provider times out, then the shopper presses Pay again. What should the service do?

Return the existing attempt's pending status, retry or look up that attempt using its original provider key, and reconcile. Do not assume timeout means failure. If the provider's deduplication window has elapsed, investigate before issuing another charge.

Follow-ups and rubric

Follow-ups: What if price changes during checkout? Who can adjust stock? What happens when refunds fail? Strong: states invariants, demonstrates conditional updates, exposes payment uncertainty, and names the recovery owner. Weak: reads cached stock as authoritative, uses a new key for every retry, or releases stock while payment status is unknown.

A short spoken walkthrough

Candidate: What should happen if payment succeeds after the hold expires?
Interviewer: Do not oversell; refund if stock cannot be obtained safely.
Candidate: I will start with the simplest durable path and state the boundary before distributing it.
Interviewer: Why not roll back the SQL transaction to reverse the charge?
Candidate: The provider is another authority. A local rollback cannot undo its committed effect. I reconcile the stable payment identity, then attempt the permitted order transition. If inventory cannot be reacquired, I create an auditable refund action with its own retry and reconciliation state, rather than calling the whole workflow atomic.
