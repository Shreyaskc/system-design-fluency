10. Add regions only with a recovery and ownership plan

Listen to this chapter →

Geography changes the problem

A region is a geographic deployment area containing infrastructure with some shared failure risks. An availability zone is an isolation area within a region under a provider's documented model. Neither term guarantees independence from all identity, networking, software, or operational failures. Corner Market starts in one city with a deployment spanning local failure domains. Another region is justified by a geographic latency requirement, a disaster-recovery objective, a residency constraint, or a business need for regional isolation.

Active-passive means one region normally serves writes while another is prepared to take over. Active-active means multiple regions serve work concurrently, but it does not specify who may write the same record. Assigning each store a home region lets many regions be active while each inventory item retains one writer. Routing an inventory reservation across a long-distance link adds latency; accepting local writes everywhere instead creates a conflict-resolution problem.

Recovery objectives are contracts

RTO states a restoration-time target. RPO states tolerated data loss. Assume an illustrative thirty-minute RTO and five-minute RPO for catalog administration; payment records may require a stricter contract. A backup every day cannot by itself satisfy five-minute RPO. Continuous log shipping might support that goal only if shipping, retention, credentials, restoration, and replay are tested together.

Break recovery into detection, decision, fencing the old writer, data promotion or restore, traffic movement, dependency reconnection, and validation. If these take 3 + 5 + 4 + 6 + 2 + 3 + 4 minutes, total recovery is 27 minutes, leaving little margin under a thirty-minute objective. DNS caching, stuck clients, or missing secrets can consume the margin. Recovery drills should measure the shopper-visible endpoint, not just the moment a database starts.

Resolve ownership before conflicts

During a regional partition, two writable copies cannot both independently promise the last apple to different customers while preserving a single shared stock invariant. Options include a single reachable owner, prior regional quota allocation, or an explicit oversell policy. Last-write-wins reconciliation discards one update based on an ordering rule; that may be acceptable for a cosmetic preference but cannot undo a fulfilled purchase. Timestamps also carry clock uncertainty and are not proof of causal order.

Failback is a second migration. After the old region returns, do not simply reopen writes there. Determine which region is authoritative, reconcile changes, rebuild or catch up replicas, verify state, then move traffic under controlled ownership. Test what happens when the original region returns while automation is still promoting the standby. Human approval may be part of a business recovery procedure, but its staffing and decision time belong in the RTO model.

Cost is a model with uncertainty

Estimate total operating cost as compute plus storage plus requests plus network transfer plus managed-service charges plus operational effort. Use current provider pricing when making a real purchasing decision; these examples deliberately avoid invented dollar prices. The catalog baseline transfers 4 GB/day before images. If an image variant adds an average 200 KB to each of two million daily page loads, that adds 400 GB/day. Media caching may matter more financially than optimizing a small orders table.

Replication multiplies stored copies, cross-region transfer, and operational testing. More services multiply deployment coordination and on-call knowledge. A managed database may cost more directly while reducing recovery and maintenance effort; quantify the staffing assumption rather than pretending engineering time is free. A cheaper design that cannot satisfy the correctness contract is not an equivalent alternative. Conversely, five-nines availability is wasteful if no user or business requirement funds its consequences.

Evolve through reversible steps

Use an expand-migrate-contract change: add a compatible new field or store, populate historical data, run both readers or compare outputs, switch traffic gradually, then remove the old path after evidence supports removal. Shadow reads compare a new implementation without making it authoritative. A canary release exposes a small traffic fraction to a change, with meaningful rollback signals. These techniques need representative traffic; a canary that never touches the rare payment path cannot validate that path.

Write a migration ledger: current owner, desired owner, cutover conditions, rollback conditions, data reconciliation, and the last reversible point. Some external actions, such as sending a shipment, cannot be undone by deploying old code. Separate a code rollback from a data repair or business compensation. Feature flags should have owners and removal dates so temporary branches do not become permanent architecture.

Practice: management asks for active-active because a region outage is frightening. What evidence do you bring?

Bring the specific customer impact, required RTO/RPO, measured current recovery time, ownership model, and estimated complexity and cost of alternatives. Compare tested active-passive recovery against partitioned active-active writes. If each store has one home writer, explain how cross-region customers behave during home-region loss. Do not promise universal write availability while concealing correctness compromises.

Exit artifact: rehearse a regional loss and a failback using a timed checklist. Every recovery objective should map to a demonstrated procedure or an explicitly recorded gap.

Request trace: One regional inventory owner

A shopper in region B reserves an item owned by region A; B forwards to A and waits within a deadline. A partition occurs after A commits. B reports pending and reconciles using the same request identity. It must not independently repeat the decrement against a writable regional copy.
