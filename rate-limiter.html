LESSON 19 / 27 · ORIGINAL TEACHING EXAMPLES

Protect the market: distributed rate limiting

Listen to this chapter →

Worked interview • correctness boundary • 45 minutes

Prompt and scope

Exact prompt: “Design a distributed rate limiter for Corner Market. Protect product browsing, merchant writes, and checkout from accidental overload and abusive clients. Explain what happens when the limiter itself fails.” A rate limiter decides whether an operation may proceed within a configured traffic budget. It does not prove that a client is trustworthy, prevent every denial-of-service attack, or reserve inventory.

Ask before drawing

  • What identity is limited? Assume authenticated account plus route class, with a separate coarse anonymous network limit. Shared network addresses must not be the sole identity for logged-in shoppers.
  • What policy? Assume browsing allows 10 requests/second with a burst of 20, merchant mutation allows 2/second with a burst of 5. Checkout has a separate admission budget and transaction safeguards.
  • How strict? Assume small temporary over-admission is acceptable for browsing, but a merchant's contracted quota must not increase merely because servers are added.
  • Where? One region initially; three regions later. Global billing quotas are a separate durable accounting problem.

Goals and estimates

Assume 100,000 daily active shoppers, 200 API requests per active shopper per day, and a peak ten times the daily average. These are interview inputs, not observed production measurements. Traffic is 20,000,000 / 86,400 = 231.48 requests/second on average; use 2,315 requests/second for peak planning. Assume 50,000 concurrently active identity/route keys. At a planning allowance of 200 bytes per key including overhead, state is about 10 MB per copy; measure the actual datastore representation before buying capacity. One atomic decision per request creates about 2,315 datastore operations/second at peak. A tenfold safety scenario is 23,150 decisions/second, not a claim that one node can sustain it.

The proposed service-level objective (SLO), a measurable reliability target, is 99.9% successful limiter decisions monthly with added latency below 5 milliseconds at the 99th percentile inside the region. A percentile says what fraction of observations finish below a threshold. Set an explicit timeout smaller than the caller's remaining deadline. Rejections should be distinguishable from dependency failures, and policy changes should become visible within one minute.

Interface, state, and diagram

Check(subject, route_class, cost=1, policy_version)
  → {allowed, remaining, retry_after_ms, reason}
HTTP rejection: 429 Too Many Requests; Retry-After in seconds
bucket key: (tenant_id, subject_hash, route_class, policy_version)
value: {tokens_scaled_integer, last_refill_ms}
policy: {rate_per_second, capacity, expires_at}

Never accept tenant identity or the policy version directly from an untrusted header. Authenticate at the gateway and derive the key there. Hashing an identifier reduces accidental exposure but does not automatically make the data anonymous. Keep raw identities out of metrics labels.

Build the smallest correct design

Start with one process and a token bucket. A token represents permission for one unit of work; the bucket refills at rate r up to capacity B. For elapsed seconds dt, compute available = min(B, old_tokens + r × dt). Admit only if available covers cost, then subtract cost. This bounds accepted work over any interval of length t by roughly B + r × t, assuming a correct monotonic elapsed-time calculation. A full browsing bucket admits 20 immediate requests. Request 21 needs 0.1 seconds of refill at 10 tokens/second.

Two gateways independently storing a full bucket would admit 40 immediate requests. Move each identity's state to a shared owner and combine read, refill, decision, and write into one atomic operation: other requests cannot observe a half-completed update. A short Redis script is one implementation option; atomic script execution does not imply durable global consensus. Use integer-scaled tokens to make rounding explicit, clamp negative elapsed time to zero, and cap large elapsed intervals at a full bucket. Prefer time obtained consistently at the state owner; wall-clock jumps still require a documented policy and tests.

Expire idle bucket state only after enough idle time to refill completely. Deleting a partially empty bucket early grants a fresh burst. Include policy version in the key only with a deliberate migration rule: changing versions resets state and can grant another burst. An alternative updates rate and capacity while preserving and clamping existing tokens. Test rollout behavior, not merely steady state.

Deep dives: contention and geography

Hash identity keys across partitions. A hot merchant remains one hot key even after adding partitions. Local token leases can amortize calls: a central owner grants a gateway a bounded number of tokens, which it spends locally. Define the extra admitted work during a failure as at most the total unspent outstanding leases, provided leases cannot be reused after reassignment. Give leases expiry and an owner generation. This spends accuracy to gain availability and latency; do not describe it as exact.

For three regions, choose a home region for strict per-account policy, accepting remote latency and reduced availability during partitions. For browsing, split a global budget into regional allocations whose sum is bounded and rebalance conservatively. Three regions each enforcing the entire nominal global budget triples the allowance. An exact global decision across a partition cannot simultaneously offer uninterrupted independent regional admission; state the business choice rather than hiding it behind a product name.

Failure analysis and alternatives

If the bucket store times out, browsing may use a deliberately smaller local emergency budget, with a metric identifying degraded decisions. This is bounded fail-open behavior: some requests continue without the normal shared decision. Merchant writes can fail closed with a retriable dependency error, subject to the product's tolerance for lost availability. Neither policy should bypass authentication, authorization, or payment correctness. A retry after an uncertain decision might consume two tokens; conservative double charging is acceptable here, but must not become a duplicate checkout charge.

Reject a fixed one-minute counter when smooth overload control matters: 100 requests at the end of one minute and 100 at the start of the next may pass almost together. A sliding log records every timestamp and offers a precise rolling window but consumes memory proportional to request volume. A sliding counter is a cheaper approximation. Token buckets fit our explicit burst contract; they do not offer a strict “no more than N in every rolling minute” promise.

Track allowed and denied counts, decision latency, store failures, hot partitions, policy age, and emergency mode usage. Alert on saturation and unexpected admission changes, not on every rejection. Set per-tenant fairness and weighted costs so a cheap metadata lookup and expensive export do not share a misleading one-request cost. Protect the limiter's own API and cap key cardinality to prevent attackers manufacturing endless keys.

Evolution and interview close

Begin with a shared regional state owner plus measured failover behavior. Add partitions after measuring load, leases only after quantifying accuracy tolerance, and regional allocation only when geography requires it. Cost includes one decision per call, replication, policy distribution, and operational complexity. Test two concurrent requests for the final token, clock rollback, policy transition, failover with lost recent state, and a single identity producing most traffic.

Practice: a gateway holds a lease of 8 tokens and crashes. What is the safe interpretation?

The central owner already deducted those 8 tokens. Losing unused tokens temporarily under-admits. Reissuing them before proving they cannot still be spent risks over-admission. A restarted gateway must not reuse an old lease generation.

Interviewer follow-ups and answer rubric

Follow-ups: How does a premium tenant get weighted service? Can a successful retry be charged twice? What exact bound is relaxed during regional failure? Strong: names the quota identity, derives burst behavior, makes state transition atomic, and quantifies degraded admission. Weak: says “put Redis at the gateway,” counts separately on every server, or promises exact global quotas and full partition availability without explaining coordination.

Technical reference checked 2026-10-19: Redis scripting documentation confirms scripts execute atomically and block other server activity while running; keep scripts bounded. All sizing, policies, and architecture choices above are fictional design assumptions.

A short spoken walkthrough

Candidate: Is the limit a strict global entitlement or regional abuse protection?
Interviewer: Begin with regional abuse protection.
Candidate: I will start with the simplest durable path and state the boundary before distributing it.
Interviewer: Can we simply deploy the same bucket in three regions?
Candidate: Only if the contract permits the multiplied allowance. For a strict global budget I would allocate bounded regional rights or coordinate through one owner; the former can strand capacity and the latter adds latency and partition unavailability. I would keep billing quotas in a separate durable accounting path.
